This is not a code of conduct. It is the unwritten culture, stated warmly — a few things to know about how we will spend our time together.

## How Sessions Work

Most sessions are conversations, not lectures. A facilitator opens with a short frame — five to ten minutes — and then the room talks. You are welcome to speak, to listen, to take notes, to ask questions, or to sit quietly and think. There is no expectation that everyone speaks. There is an expectation that everyone who wants to speak gets room to.

Some sessions are workshops: hands-on, making-oriented, more structure and less open discussion. The facilitator will tell you what you are making and why, and then you will make it — alone or in a small group, depending on the session.

Morning circles open each day in the main hall. They are short, warm, and everyone is together. The host sets the tone for the day — what to expect, what is different, what to pay attention to.

Closing circles at the end of each day are optional to speak and good to witness. The host invites a few people to share a reflection. Some of the best closings are mostly silence and nodding.

## Breaks Are Part of the Rhythm

Breaks are not gaps between sessions. They are part of the flow. Each break is long enough for a real conversation or a real rest — usually half an hour. Coffee, tea, and snacks are always available in the kitchen.

The quiet lounge is open all day, every day. It is a space for reading, thinking, stepping away from the energy, or catching up on messages. No conversation is expected in the quiet lounge. That is the point.

## Meals Are Together

Lunch is served each day in the kitchen and main hall, family-style. Grab a plate and sit next to someone you have not talked to yet — or someone you have, and want to keep talking to.

On day two, lunch is a working lunch: each table has a question card that connects to the morning sessions. Talk if you want. Eat in silence if you need to. The questions are an invitation, not a requirement.

Dietary needs noted during registration have been shared with the kitchen. If something is missing or wrong, find the welcome host — they will sort it out.

## What Is Optional

Every session is optional. Every evening gathering is optional. Every conversation is optional. You are an adult who knows what you need.

If you need to step out of a session, step out. If you need to skip a session to rest, skip it. If you need a walk, a nap, or an hour alone, take it. The summit is designed so you can participate fully without having to perform constantly.

The quiet lounge and the neighbourhood (section 8) are always available.

## Morning and Evening Rituals

We open each day together in the main hall — a short circle, everyone present, the host framing the day. It is the one moment each day when everyone is in the same room at the same time. It lasts ten to fifteen minutes.

We close each day together too — a reflection circle, lighter than the morning, more about what the day left us with than what comes next.

On day three, the closing gathering is the final circle. The host invites anyone who wants to speak to share something: a reflection, a thank-you, a commitment, a question to carry forward. Then a few words from the host, and the official close of the summit.

## Notes, Photos, and Sharing

Take notes however you like. Share them if you want to. Photographs are welcome — of the space, of the sessions, of the moments between sessions. If you are photographing people, ask first. It takes two seconds and it matters.

If you share anything publicly — notes, photos, reflections — the summit has no official hashtag and no branding guidelines. Share what felt meaningful to you, in your own words. That is enough.
