You will know you are in the right place when you see the green door. It is the only green door on Summit Street — an old brick building, east side of the block, halfway down. You cannot miss it.

Push it open. It is unlocked from 8:15 am. The entry hall is small, warm-lit, with a bench and hooks along the right wall. The welcome desk is ahead on your left, past a glass inner door. Someone will be there to greet you by name.

## The First Few Minutes

Check-in takes about a minute. You will get your badge and a small printed programme card. The welcome host will point out where to drop your coat, where the coffee is, and where the restrooms are — the things you actually want to know in the first thirty seconds after arriving.

After that, the time is yours. Coffee is in the kitchen, to the left of the main hall. The quiet lounge is open if you need a moment. The morning circle begins at 9:15 in the main hall — until then, settle in at your own pace, find a comfortable spot, and say hello to whoever is already there.

## The Rhythm of the First Day

The first day is paced for arrival. You are not expected to be at full energy by 9 am. The morning eases in — a welcome circle, a light activity to get people talking, a walkthrough of how the summit works. The afternoon gives you choices: parallel sessions in the main hall and workshop room, a long break, a closing circle to reflect. The evening is a welcome gathering at the office — drinks, food, music, no agenda.

If you arrive feeling tired from travel, that is expected. The day is designed so you can participate fully without having to perform.

The full [day one schedule]({{< ref "/schedule/day-1" >}}) has the details. The [entry sequence]({{< ref "/arriving/entry-sequence" >}}) page walks through the door-to-desk path step by step. And section 7, [How We Gather]({{< ref "/how-we-gather/rhythms-rituals" >}}), covers what to expect from sessions, breaks, and the unwritten culture of the summit.

## What It Will Feel Like

It will feel like a group of people who are genuinely glad to be in the same room. It will feel warm but not forced. Structured but not scripted. There will be coffee, and natural light, and a few moments of quiet between the conversations.

Some people will know each other. Some people will know no one. By the end of the first morning, that distinction will have mostly dissolved.

{{< callout text="The best version of a summit does not feel like an event being run. It feels like a place being made, together, by the people in it." >}}

You are part of that. Arrive when you can. Bring what you need. Everything else is already here, waiting for you.
