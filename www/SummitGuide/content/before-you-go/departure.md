The summit ends at 4 pm on day three. The closing circle is the last time everyone is in the room together. After that, the building stays open for an hour — enough time to gather your things, say your goodbyes, and head out at your own pace.

## The Last Day

Day three starts like the others — morning circle at 9 am in the main hall. The host sets the tone: this is the last day, and it is shorter than the first two. There is space in the schedule for closing conversations and a final reflection.

The afternoon session ends at 3:30 pm. There is a short break — coffee, a stretch, a moment to process — and then we gather for the closing circle at 3:45 pm.

The closing circle is the final gathering. The host invites anyone who wants to speak to share something: a reflection, a thank-you, a commitment, a question to carry forward. Speaking is optional. Staying until the end is not required — if you need to leave earlier for travel, slip out quietly. No one will call attention to it.

After the host's closing words — short, warm, and personal — the summit is officially over. The room tends to linger. That is expected. Goodbyes take the time they take.

## Getting Home

The building closes at 5 pm on day three. The welcome desk can call you a taxi or point you toward the transit stop. [Section 2]({{< ref "/getting-here/transit-access" >}}) has the full transit details — Central Station is a five-minute walk from the office, and buses run every ten minutes along Summit Street.

If you are heading to the airport, give yourself an hour. Traffic is light in the afternoon. If you are heading to the train station, the walk is short and the pavement is smooth all the way — no stairs, no cobblestones.

## Before You Leave the Building

A short checklist — four things to glance at before you walk out the green door.

**Your belongings.** Check the coat room. Check under your chair in the main hall. Check the windowsill in the workshop room. Check the kitchen — people leave water bottles there more than anywhere else.

**Your notebook.** If you took notes, make sure they are in your bag. If you used a shared notebook in the workshop room, it stays — the pages will be photographed and shared afterward.

**The lost-and-found box.** It lives at the welcome desk through the end of the month. If you realise you left something after you have gone, email the host. They will check the box and mail it to you.

**A last look.** The main hall at the end of a summit is a particular kind of quiet. Take a moment in it if you want to.

## Support and Emergency Information

This section is kept short so you can find it quickly. It is also in the footer of every page.

**In an emergency — any emergency — dial your local emergency number.** The office address is 42 Summit Street, Downtown District. If you need help finding the words, hand your phone to the closest staff member.

**Summit emergency contact:** +1-555-0123. This number reaches someone in the building for the full duration of the summit, from doors-open on day one to lock-up on day three. Call it if you are lost, hurt, unwell, or unsure what to do. It is staffed, not recorded.

**First aid kit:** In the kitchen, above the sink, in a red box marked with a white cross. The welcome host can also get it for you.

**The closest hospital** is Downtown General, a ten-minute drive from the office. The welcome host can arrange transport.

**The quiet lounge** is always available if you need to sit down, be still, or step away. No explanation required.

## After the Summit

The guide stays online. The URL will not change. Come back to it when you want to look something up — a session description, a name from the people page, a transit note for next time.

**Session notes and materials** will be compiled in the week after the summit and shared by email to everyone who attended. If you took photos or notes you want to include, send them to the host. Attribution is optional — anonymous contributions are welcome.

**Recordings**, if any sessions were recorded, will be shared in the same follow-up email. Not every session is recorded — the host will note which ones at the start of each day.

**Staying in touch** is up to you. There is no official alumni group, no required channel, no mailing list you will be added to without asking. If you met people you want to keep talking to, exchange contact details the old-fashioned way. The last hour after closing circle is good for this.

## A Closing Note

You made it to the last page of the guide.

That means you moved through the full arc — from packing your bag to finding the green door, from your first morning circle to your last closing reflection, from "who are all these people" to "I know where the quiet lounge is and I am glad it exists."

The summit was designed to be a particular kind of gathering: warm without being performative, structured without being rigid, social without being exhausting. If it felt that way — if you felt welcomed, if you felt able to participate on your own terms, if you left with a question you are still thinking about — then the design worked.

If it did not feel that way, tell the host. Feedback lands differently when it comes from someone who was in the room. The next summit is better because of what you say.

The green door is the last thing you see on the way out. It looks the same as it did on the way in. You are not.

Travel safely. Stay in touch. Come back next time.
