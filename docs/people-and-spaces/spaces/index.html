The Spaces and Their Character

The rooms you will spend your time in — each with its own character, unwritten rules, and way of holding people.

The office is not vast. Four rooms you will come back to, each with a different temperature of activity. The floor plan has them all marked with numbered callouts. Here they are in use, not empty.

The Main Hall — Callout 3

The main hall is where we gather. Morning circles, closing circles, evening gatherings, and the sessions that want everyone in one room.

It is an open, high-ceilinged room with tall windows along one wall. The light changes through the day — soft in the morning, full in the afternoon, amber by closing circle. Chairs are arranged in a loose arc for circles and can be reconfigured for sessions. There are cushions along the window ledge and a few armchairs at the back for anyone who prefers them.

The floor is level and the entrance is wide and step-free. If you use a wheelchair or have mobility needs, the space near the front left of the arc is kept open — it has the clearest sight lines and the most room to move. The welcome host can show you in through the side entrance if the main doors feel busy.

Unwritten rule: The front row is for people who want to be close to the conversation. The back is for people who want to listen quietly. Both are welcome. Neither is judged.

The main hall has a way of holding silence that makes people want to speak into it. It is the kind of room that listens. — An observation, not a design specification

The Workshop Room — Callout 4

The workshop room is the hands-on counterpart to the main hall. It is smaller, warmer, and meant for making and moving.

Tables are on castors and rearrange easily. The walls are writable — floor-to-ceiling whiteboard panels you can draw on with the markers in the tray. There is a screen on the far wall for anyone who needs to share their screen. The chairs are stackable and the room reshapes in under two minutes.

The entrance is step-free. A wide doorway connects to the main corridor. Tables are height-adjustable — if you need a workspace at a different height, ask the welcome host before the session and it will be set up when you arrive.

Unwritten rule: Leave the room the way you found it, or better. Push your chair in. Cap the markers. The next session starts in fifteen minutes and someone else is about to make something in here.

The Quiet Lounge — Callout 5

The quiet lounge is a small room at the end of the corridor, away from the main hall and kitchen. It is the one room in the building with a door that closes — and closing it is encouraged.

Inside: a sofa, two armchairs, a low bookshelf, and a window that looks out onto the side garden. The lighting is softer than the rest of the office. There is a small side table with a carafe of water and a glass.

No conversation is expected here. It is not a social room. It is a room for reading, thinking, napping, journaling, catching up on messages, or simply being still in the middle of a full day. You do not need to explain why you are there.

The quiet lounge is step-free. The door is wide enough for a wheelchair and the furniture can be repositioned — ask the welcome host if you need the layout adjusted.

Unwritten rule: If the door is closed, someone is inside. Come back later. If you go in and leave the door open, the next person knows there is room.

QUIET LOUNGE Open all day, every day. No sign-up, no time limit, no questions asked. The quiet lounge exists because being around people all day is a lot, and sometimes you need twenty minutes with a window and your own thoughts.

The Kitchen — Callout 6

The kitchen is the unofficial gathering spot. Coffee, tea, and light snacks are available all day. Lunch is served here — buffet-style, family seating that spills into the main hall.

It has a long counter, a commercial coffee machine, a fridge stocked with milk and alternatives, and a table that seats eight. More chairs than seats, more mugs than chairs, and a dishwasher that runs roughly constantly.

The kitchen is step-free and the counter has a lowered section for reach. If you cannot find something — a particular milk, a snack that was supposed to be out, a clean mug — check the cupboard to the left of the sink. If it is not there either, find the kitchen keeper (described in People You Will Meet).

Unwritten rule: The kitchen is where the best conversations happen between sessions. It is also where people go to be alone with a cup of tea for five minutes. Both things are true. Read the room.

The Restrooms

Restrooms are in the corridor between the kitchen and the quiet lounge — marked on the floor plan with the restroom icon. There are two, both individual rooms with full-height doors and locking handles. One is marked for accessible use and has grab bars, a lower sink, and enough turning space for a wheelchair.

The restrooms are all-gender. Use whichever is free.

The Coat Room and Entrance

The coat room is the first space you enter — a short corridor with hooks along the right wall, a bench underneath, and space for bags and coats. It is just past the main entrance and before the welcome desk. Drop your things, take what you need, and travel light through the day.

The entrance has a ramp from the pavement to the door. The inner glass door is held open during the day. If you arrive and the outer door is closed, push — it opens inward and the welcome desk is straight ahead.

The building was built before accessibility was a design constraint. It has been adapted with care. If something does not work for you, say so — adjustments are easy and the team wants to make them.

Every room on this page is step-free. The building has a lift. If a route is not obvious, the welcome host will walk you through it.
