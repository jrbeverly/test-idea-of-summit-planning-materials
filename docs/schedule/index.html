The Schedule

Three days of sessions, breaks, meals, and gatherings — presented as a timeline, not a grid.

Here is how the next three days will unfold. The schedule is presented as a timeline — mornings are together, afternoons diverge, evenings are for gathering. Each day has its own rhythm.

Session Types
  • Talk or presentation
  • Workshop or interactive session
  • Break period
  • Meal period
  • Social event or gathering
  • Coffee and beverage station

The Three Days

Day 1: Arrive and Orient — A lighter day. Morning arrival and orientation, afternoon sessions, and an evening welcome gathering. The day eases in.

Day 2: Dig In — The core day. Morning deep-dives, a working lunch, afternoon workshops, and a long evening gathering. This is where the summit finds its rhythm.

Day 3: Close and Depart — The closing day. Morning synthesis, a final circle, lunch, and a departure window. Lighter, shorter, reflective.

Day 1: Arrive and Orient

A lighter first day focused on arrival, orientation, and settling in.

The first day is paced for arrival. You are not expected to be at full energy by 9am. The morning eases in. The afternoon gives you options. The evening is for gathering without agenda.

If you arrive feeling tired from travel, that is expected. The day is designed so you can participate fully without having to perform. Show up. Listen. Join when you want to.

  1. 8:30
    Doors Open The main hall is open and the coffee is on. Arrive, drop your bag in the coat room, and settle in. There is no rush — this first half-hour is for arriving at your own pace.
  2. 9:00
    Welcome Circle We gather in the main hall. Our host opens the summit with a short welcome, sets the tone for the next three days, and invites everyone to introduce themselves — name, where you came from, and one thing you are curious about.
  3. 9:45
    Getting to Know Each Other A light, structured activity designed to break the ice without making anyone cringe. You will be in small groups of four or five. No trust falls, no role-playing. Just good questions and a reason to talk to people you have not met yet.
  4. 10:45
    Morning Break Stretch, refill your coffee, step outside for air. The kitchen has pastries and fruit if you are hungry. The quiet lounge is open if you need a moment away from the group.
  5. 11:15
    How These Three Days Work A short walkthrough of the summit rhythm: how sessions run, where things are, what is optional and what is together. You will learn how to read the schedule (you are looking at it), how to find your way (section 4 has you covered), and who to ask if you need anything (section 6).
  6. 12:30
    Lunch Catered lunch in the kitchen and main hall. Seating is family-style — grab a plate and sit next to someone you have not talked to yet. The food is good. Dietary needs noted during registration have been accounted for.
  7. 14:00
    Afternoon Sessions Two parallel sessions in the main hall and workshop room. Topics will be posted on the board by the kitchen the morning of. Sessions are conversational, not lecture-style — the facilitator opens with a short frame, then the room talks. You are welcome to switch between sessions.
  8. 15:30
    Afternoon Break Coffee, tea, and a chance to process the afternoon before we gather again. The kitchen tends to be where the best hallway conversations happen.
  9. 16:00
    Closing Circle We come back together in the main hall to reflect on the day. The host invites a few voices to share: something that surprised you, a conversation you are still thinking about, a question you are carrying into tomorrow. This is optional to speak, good to witness.
  10. 17:00
    Free Time A gap to rest, walk, or explore the neighbourhood (see section 8 for local tips). The office stays open. The quiet lounge is always available.
  11. 18:30
    Welcome Gathering An informal evening at the office. Drinks, light food, music, and no agenda. Bring yourself. Stay as long as you like. This is the first of two evening gatherings — low-key, high-warmth, entirely what you make of it.

Day 2: Dig In

The core day — morning sessions, working lunch, afternoon deep-dives, and an evening gathering.

Day two is the densest day. It stretches from morning coffee to a late evening gathering, and it asks more of you than yesterday did — more thinking, more talking, more making. It is also the day where the summit finds its rhythm.

Pace yourself. The breaks are there for a reason. Not every session needs your full attention. Some of the best moments happen in the gaps.

  1. 8:30
    Coffee and Catch-Up The office is open early. Coffee is ready. Use this time to revisit a conversation from yesterday that you want to continue before the day begins.
  2. 9:00
    Morning Kickoff The host opens the day with a short framing: what today is about, what is different from yesterday, and what to pay attention to. Five minutes. Then we move.
  3. 9:15
    Deep-Dive Sessions Two parallel sessions running in the main hall and workshop room. These are the longer, meatier sessions — ninety minutes each, designed for real exploration rather than survey. Each session has a facilitator, a short opening prompt, and a lot of time for the room to think together. You picked your track at breakfast.
  4. 10:45
    Morning Break Pastries, fruit, coffee, and the inevitable clustering of people who just spent ninety minutes thinking together and have more to say. The break runs a full half-hour — long enough for a real conversation, not just a refill.
  5. 11:15
    Parallel Tracks Continue The second round of deep-dive sessions. Same format, different topics. If you were in the main hall for the first block, consider switching to the workshop room for a change of setting and perspective. Or stay — the deep-dive is designed to work as a two-part arc.
  6. 12:30
    Working Lunch Lunch is served in the kitchen, but eating is not the only agenda. Each table has a question on a card — a conversation starter that connects to the morning sessions. Talk if you want. Eat in silence if you need to. The questions are an invitation, not a requirement.
  7. 14:00
    Afternoon Workshops The afternoon shifts from discussion to making. Workshops are hands-on: write, sketch, prototype, plan. Each workshop has a concrete output — a doc, a diagram, a decision, a thing you can point to. Facilitators provide just enough structure. The room provides the rest.
  8. 15:30
    Afternoon Break Coffee, a stretch, and a moment to check in with yourself. The quiet lounge is there if you need it. You have been thinking hard — a short reset before the final session makes a difference.
  9. 16:00
    Show and Tell We gather in the main hall. Each workshop group shares what they made or discovered — not a formal presentation, more like showing a friend something you just built. Five minutes per group, light facilitation, genuine curiosity from the room.
  10. 17:30
    Free Time A longer gap before the evening. Rest, walk, call home, or find a quiet corner to process the day. The neighbourhood has good food if you want to step out (section 8 has recommendations).
  11. 19:00
    Evening Gathering A longer, looser evening than yesterday. Dinner is served. Someone might bring out a guitar. Someone else might start a fire in the courtyard if the weather holds. Stay late or leave early — the gathering is here if you want it, and no one is counting heads.

Day 3: Close and Depart

The closing day — morning wrap-up, final sessions, and departure.

Day three is the shortest day and the lightest. The morning holds the final sessions and the closing gathering. The afternoon is for departing at your own pace.

If you feel a little sad as the summit ends — that is normal. These three days are designed to feel like a small world. Leaving a small world takes a moment. Take yours.

  1. 9:00
    Coffee and Conversation A later start — you have earned it. Coffee is on. The room is quieter than yesterday. Conversations are reflective. People are starting to process what has happened over the past two days.
  2. 9:30
    Morning Circle We gather one last time in the main hall. The host frames the closing day: what we are here to do, what closure looks like, and how to carry what we have built beyond these three days. Short, warm, forward-looking.
  3. 10:00
    Final Sessions The last round of sessions. These are shorter than yesterday — sixty minutes — and focused on synthesis: what did we learn, what do we want to keep doing, what is the smallest next step. The output is not a deliverable. It is clarity.
  4. 11:00
    Morning Break Pastries, coffee, and the beginning of goodbyes. Some people will leave after the closing gathering. Others will stay into the afternoon. This break is a good moment to exchange contact details before the room starts to thin.
  5. 11:30
    Closing Gathering We come together in the main hall for the final time. The host invites anyone who wants to speak to share: a reflection, a thank-you, a commitment, a question to carry forward. No pressure. Some of the best closings are mostly silence and nodding. Then: a few words from the host, an invitation to stay in touch, and the official close of the summit.
  6. 12:30
    Lunch and Goodbyes One last meal together. The kitchen has food. People drift in and out. Some leave directly from lunch. Others stay for the afternoon to help tidy up or continue a conversation that needs more time. There is no formal end. You leave when you are ready.
  7. 14:00
    Departure Window The office stays open until 4pm. If you have a later train or flight, you are welcome to stay — the quiet lounge is open, the Wi-Fi is on, the coffee is still hot. Lost something? Check the lost-and-found by the welcome desk. Section 9 has notes on what comes next.
